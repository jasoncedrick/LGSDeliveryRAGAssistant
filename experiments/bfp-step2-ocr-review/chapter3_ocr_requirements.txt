The raw government corpus contains inherent source biases that impact retrieval:
Uneven Service Coverage.
Frequently transacted procedures dominate the text, yield-
ing vastly more usable chunks, whereas uncommon procedural exceptions possess little
usable evidence
Formal Institutional Language.
Official phrasing heavily overrides local spelling
variants, mixed-language terminology, and colloquial inquiries.
Time and Versioning Biases.
Legacy versions of the charter contain outdated fees,
timelines, and office locations. Superseded versions are explicitly isolated from the active
answer corpus but retained in a change log.
To mitigate digitization failures, the text extraction pipeline applies the following controls:
Artifact Removal.
Machine-readable text is extracted, and OCR is applied to scanned
pages. Repeated headers, page artifacts, and accidental whitespace are stripped, while
critical procedural conditions, numerical figures, negation terms, and units are strictly
preserved.
Tabular Integrity Verification.
Procedural tables detailing rows, processing fees,
required documents, and turnaround times are cross-checked manually against the original
page images. Extraction mistakes are corrected directly; the system strictly forbids the
use of an LLM to hallucinate or reconstruct missing text.
Deduplication.
Duplicate passages are removed while retaining their source links.
Valid low-frequency exceptions are preserved; the pipeline explicitly avoids discarding
valid official material simply to equalize the number of chunks per service category.
The cleaned text must be segmented into source-linked chunks that preserve proce-
dural context while conforming to the dense embedding model’s architectural constraints.
Because the paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 model imposes a default 128-
token sequence limit, chunk boundaries are defined by actual token lengths rather than
arbitrary character counts to prevent unnoticed truncation of critical policy data during
vectorization. Specifically, the corpus is segmented into uniform chunks of 120 tokens with
a 20-token overlap using a HuggingFace tokenizer-aware splitter, preserving procedural
continuity across chunk boundaries.
The system does not employ synthetic feature generation or random oversam-
pling of official passages to balance the database as this architecture performs Retrieval-
Augmented Generation rather than supervised classification. Instead, structural imbal-
ance is addressed through stratified query selection during evaluation. The system eval-
uation benchmark enforces an equal quota of underlying scenario questions across all
target services, ensuring that high-volume administrative categories do not skew the final
performance metrics.
